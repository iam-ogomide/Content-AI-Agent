## 2. Audience

Content speaks to multiple segments depending on channel:
- **Lenders & fintechs (B2B)** — banks, microfinance institutions, digital lenders integrating credit data into their products. Care about: reliability, coverage/data depth, integration ease, compliance.
- **Consumers / borrowers (B2C)** — individuals checking or building a credit profile. Care about: "what does this mean for me," trust, simplicity, no jargon.
- **Partners / investors** — care about scale, traction, market credibility.

Default: identify the segment from the brief's `audience` input. If not specified, ask rather than guessing — B2B and B2C copy should not sound identical.

## 3. Tone: Approachable & Clear

- Plain language over jargon. If a technical/financial term is unavoidable (e.g. "credit bureau data," "KYC"), explain it in the same sentence the first time it appears.
- Friendly expert, not corporate — write like a knowledgeable colleague, not a press release.
- Confident but not hype-driven. Make claims we can back with specifics (numbers, concrete outcomes) rather than superlatives.
- Warm, never condescending — especially in consumer-facing content about credit, which can be a sensitive/anxiety-inducing topic for people.

## 4. Vocabulary

**Prefer:**
- "credit profile," "credit history," "verify," "assess creditworthiness"
- Active voice, short sentences, concrete numbers over vague claims
- Second person ("you," "your business") to speak directly to the reader

**Avoid:**
- Jargon without explanation: "underwriting," "bureau pull," "API-first" (unless audience is confirmed technical/B2B and term is defined)
- Hype words: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "disrupt," "unlock" (overused in fintech marketing)
- Anything implying guaranteed loan approval or credit outcomes — legal/compliance risk in financial content
- Fear-based framing around credit ("don't get rejected again") — approachable tone means reassuring, not alarming

## 5. Writing principles

1. Lead with the reader's problem or goal, not the product feature.
2. One idea per post/paragraph — don't cram multiple CTAs or messages into one piece.
3. Always end with a clear, single CTA when the brief specifies one.
4. Numbers and specifics beat adjectives ("scores 2M+ profiles" beats "massive database").
5. Never state a compliance/legal claim (data privacy, regulatory approval, guaranteed accuracy) unless it's sourced from an approved brief — flag it for human review instead of inventing it.

## 6. Do / Don't examples

**Do:**
> "Checking a borrower's credit history shouldn't take days. With CreditChek, lenders get a verified credit profile in seconds — so you can approve good borrowers faster."

**Don't:**
> "Revolutionize your lending game with our game-changing, disruptive credit API that unlocks unprecedented insights!"

## 7. Channel notes

- **LinkedIn**: slightly more formal, B2B-leaning by default, can reference industry trends/data.
- **X/Twitter**: shorter, punchier, still clear — no jargon just because it's terse.
- **Instagram**: most visual/human, captions can be warmer and more casual.
- **Email**: direct, benefit-first subject lines, one CTA.
- **Blog**: room to explain concepts fully — this is where jargon *can* be introduced, as long as it's defined.
